Step 1: Identify Asset

What asset are you assessing? (e.g., Customer Database, Web Server, Employee Data)

Asset Types

  • Data: Customer records, intellectual property, financial data.
  • Software: Applications, operating systems, firmware.

{% elif step == 2 %}

Step 2: Identify Adversarial Threat Source

Who is the adversary? (e.g., Hacktivist, Insider, Competitor, Nation State)

Common Adversarial Threat Sources

  • Individual: Outsider, Insider, Trusted Insider, Privileged Insider.
  • Group: Ad hoc, Established, Cyber Crime, Terrorist.
  • Organization: Competitor, Supplier, Partner, Customer.
  • Nation-State: Intelligence Service, Military, Proxy.
  • Vendor/Supplier (SCRM): Third-party software, cloud provider, hardware supplier.

Supply Chain Risk Note: Vendor/supplier threats include compromised software updates, malicious dependencies, or cloud service breaches.

Step 3: Identify Threat Event

What is the event? (e.g., Phishing attack, SQL Injection, Malware)

Common Threat Events

  • Reconnaissance: Scanning, Social Engineering.
  • Access: Brute Force, Exploit Vulnerability, Phishing.
  • Misuse: Privilege Escalation, Data Exfiltration.
  • Modification: Defacement, Integrity Violation.
  • Denial of Service: Flooding, Resource Exhaustion.

{% elif step == 4 %}

Step 4: Adversary Capability

What is the level of resources and expertise available to the adversary?

Capability Levels

  • Very Low: Little/no technical expertise; uses standard tools.
  • Low: Some expertise; uses available tools.
  • Moderate: Competent; can customize tools.
  • High: Highly skilled; creates custom tools.
  • Very High: State-sponsored; advanced persistent threat.
{% elif step == 5 %}

Step 5: Adversary Intent

What is the motivation and willingness of the adversary?

Intent Levels

  • Very Low: Unlikely to target; accidental.
  • Low: Limited motivation; opportunistic.
  • Moderate: Motivated; will target if goals met.
  • High: Highly motivated; persistent targeting.
  • Very High: Extremely motivated; stops at nothing.
{% elif step == 6 %}

Step 6: Adversary Targeting

How focused is the adversary on specific assets or the organization?

Targeting Levels

  • Very Low: Broad, indiscriminate (e.g., spam).
  • Low: Sector/Industry wide, not specific org.
  • Moderate: Specific organizations or assets.
  • High: Focused on critical assets/individuals.
  • Very High: Persistent, multi-vector targeting.
{% elif step == 7 %}

Step 7: Likelihood of Initiation

Based on Capability, Intent, and Targeting, how likely is the threat to be initiated?

Likelihood of Initiation

  • Very Low: Adversary is unlikely to initiate.
  • Low: Adversary may initiate.
  • Moderate: Adversary is likely to initiate.
  • High: Adversary is highly likely to initiate.
  • Very High: Adversary will almost certainly initiate.
{% elif step == 8 %}

Step 8: Vulnerability & Likelihood of Impact

What vulnerability is being exploited, and how likely is it to succeed?

Vulnerability & Impact Likelihood

  • Very Low: Vulnerability is theoretical; high defense.
  • Low: Difficult to exploit; moderate defense.
  • Moderate: Feasible to exploit; limited defense.
  • High: Easy to exploit; weak defense.
  • Very High: Trivial to exploit; no defense.
Supported formats: Nmap XML, Nessus CSV, Generic JSON.
